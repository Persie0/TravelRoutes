# Hammock overnighting guide

## Recommendation
For the Finland section, use a **hammock + tarp + insect net** as the normal sleep setup and keep the rental car mainly for transport and secure gear storage. Always follow the rules of the specific land area or national park.

## Country-by-country
- **Finland:** Everyman's Rights generally permit temporary overnight stays where access is otherwise allowed. Official Luontoon guidance explicitly discusses hammocks. Protected areas and national parks can have additional restrictions, so use designated sites where required.
- **Estonia:** RMK's freedom-to-roam guidance is permissive for short stays, but protected-area rules still apply. RMK campsites around Lahemaa are the easiest low-impact option.
- **Latvia:** Latvian State Forests (LVM) permits short-term overnight recreation in suitable state-forest locations subject to local and protected-area rules. LVM also publishes guidance for hammock overnighting.
- **Lithuania:** plan around official campgrounds and designated overnight sites, especially in protected areas such as the Curonian Spit.
- **Poland:** use official **Zanocuj w lesie** forest zones rather than assuming general Nordic-style roaming rights.

## Sleep system
For July/August take wide tree straps, a proper rain tarp, mosquito net, underquilt or insulated mat, sleeping bag/top quilt, dry sleep clothes and a stove. For normal August a sleep system around **+5°C comfort** is a reasonable starting point; if the route reaches September in Lapland, bring a noticeably warmer system, potentially around **0°C comfort** depending on your personal cold tolerance and forecast.

## Low-impact setup
Use wide straps and never damage bark. Choose healthy trees, avoid dead branches overhead, keep away from homes and cultivated land, pack out all waste, and check current wildfire and park notices. A place that is legal for an overnight hammock is not automatically a legal place to park a rental car.
