# Transport and Route Optimization

Research snapshot: **2026-08-24**. Exact timetables/fares should be rechecked after final dates are chosen.

## Recommended mode split

### Phase 1 — Netherlands / Belgium / Lille / Rouen: rail

Do **not** rent a car in the Netherlands or Flemish city section. Parking, city restrictions and hotel parking erase most of the benefit while trains are frequent and city-centre to city-centre.

Benchmarks from current route research:

| Leg / benchmark | Best general mode | Approx. duration | Notes |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Amsterdam → Ghent | train | ~2 h 15 | Rome2Rio currently surfaces Eurostar + Belgian Railways as fastest general option; useful benchmark even though the actual itinerary starts in Haarlem/Rotterdam |
| Bruges → Lille | bus or train | ~55 min bus / ~1 h 49 train | Bus can be faster; compare on the chosen date |
| Lille → Rouen | train | ~2 h 45 | direct/near-direct SNCF is efficient |
| Lille → Rouen via Amiens | train | ~3 h 33 | roughly <1 h extra transport versus direct, making Amiens a very efficient sightseeing through-stop |

### Phase 2 — Normandy / Brittany: rental car

Pick up **Rouen late Day 14 or early Day 15**. This is the point at which the car begins buying a lot of sightseeing time rather than merely adding cost.

The car is especially valuable for:

- Étretat viewpoints/trailheads;
- Honfleur without awkward rail geometry;
- D-Day beaches and cemeteries;
- Mont-Saint-Michel at early/late hours;
- Dinan + Cap Fréhel + Fort La Latte in one day;
- Ploumanac'h / Pink Granite Coast;
- Crozon Peninsula;
- Pointe Saint-Mathieu;
- Locronan;
- Carnac / Quiberon flexibility.

Return the car in **Nantes**. A one-way fee may apply, but for ~12–13 car days the route efficiency usually justifies it. Compare total rental + one-way fee + parking against a same-station return only after exact dates are known.

## Why Brest should not be the endpoint

Current route research shows **no nonstop Brest (BES) → Vienna (VIE)**. Finishing there therefore usually means either a connecting flight or repositioning by land.

Current Rome2Rio benchmark:

- **Brest → Nantes:** ~3 h 22 by train; ~4 h by bus.

That makes the logical strategy: enjoy Brest/Crozon, continue through Quimper/Carnac/Vannes, return the car in Nantes and fly from NTE when the schedule works.

If Nantes flights are bad on your exact date, Paris is the robust backup because Nantes/Le Mans/Rennes have strong TGV access to Paris and Paris has abundant Vienna options.

## Maastricht decision

**Main route: skip. Variant: add +2 days.**

Why:

1. The main trip is a west/southwest flow.
2. Maastricht pulls you southeast from Rotterdam.
3. You then have to reverse west toward Ghent/Bruges.
4. Because Brussels, Antwerp and Liège are already known, there is less opportunity to turn Maastricht into a broader east-Belgium cluster.

This is not because Maastricht is weak; it is because it has poor **value per route-hour** in this particular itinerary.

## Other Flemish cities

### Best additions for this route

- **Ypres (Ieper): A/S if WWI interest** — directly supports the westward route toward Lille.
- **Kortrijk: B** — easy half-day/meal stop; handsome centre, but not worth displacing Ghent/Bruges.

### Good cities but poor geometry here

- **Leuven: A-** — excellent university/Gothic city, but east of the line and close to Brussels, which you already know.
- **Mechelen: B+/A-** — attractive and easy by rail, but also back toward the Antwerp/Brussels corridor.

## Netherlands additions

- **Utrecht:** best +1 day extension; high value and easy rail.
- **Maastricht:** +2 days if specifically desired.
- **Middelburg/Veere/Zeeland:** +1–2 days for coastal/Delta Works variety; cross-border public transport into Belgium is less elegant than the main rail corridor.

## Transfer principles

- Use early/late trains for short city moves so sightseeing daylight is not wasted.
- Do not build the route around saving €10–20 on a bus if it creates a bad arrival or forces a hotel far from the centre.
- In Normandy/Brittany, prioritize weather windows over rigid pre-booked attraction order; shift Crozon/Granite Coast days when needed.
- Avoid driving inside the historic centres of Ghent/Bruges/Lille/Rouen when rail works better.
- Keep the final Nantes day somewhat flexible so a limited-weekly direct flight can be matched without sacrificing an S-tier coastal day.

## Route-quality audit

- **Backtracking:** low after removing Maastricht from the main route.
- **Worst transfer:** none severe; Lille→Rouen is the longest rail city transfer, and Amiens converts part of it into sightseeing.
- **Border crossings:** Schengen internal travel; no routine immigration stop, though ID must still be carried.
- **Car usefulness:** low before Rouen, very high after Rouen.
- **Best exit hub after Brittany:** Nantes first, Paris backup, Brest only if a surprisingly good connection appears.
