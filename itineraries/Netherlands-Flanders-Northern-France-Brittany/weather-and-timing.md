## Best choice among your months

### 1. September — **9/10 overall**

Best balance of:

- lower crowd pressure than July/August;
- comfortable city/hiking temperatures;
- still-good daylight early/mid month;
- generally better accommodation value;
- direct-flight shoulder-season possibilities.

Risk: western Brittany becomes more Atlantic and changeable as the month progresses. Put the most exposed Crozon/Granite Coast days on flexible bookings where possible.

### 2. July — **8.5/10**

Best for long daylight and coastal hiking reliability. The main downside is tourist density and higher lodging/rental prices.

### 3. August — **7.5/10**

Weather can be excellent, but this is the French holiday peak. Mont-Saint-Michel, Saint-Malo, Brittany coast and rental-car inventory are all more pressured. If August is chosen, favor late August over the first half when possible.

### 4. February — **4.5/10**

Works for a cheaper **city-heavy** trip, but not for the same itinerary value:

- short daylight;
- more wind/rain exposure on cliffs;
- muddy/wet hiking conditions;
- some seasonal services reduced;
- coastal sunsets/long outdoor days lose much of their advantage.

A February version should cut some far-west nature and add museums/urban stops instead.

## Climate anchors

- KNMI 1991–2020 September normal at De Bilt: about **14.7°C mean**, **77.9 mm precipitation**, **152.8 sunshine hours**.
- KNMI normal maximum temperatures fall from around **20.2°C in early September to ~18°C late September** across the Netherlands normal table.
- Belgian KMI 1991–2020 September normal at Uccle is around **19.5°C average maximum**, **11.3°C minimum**, **65.3 mm precipitation**, and ~154 sunshine hours.
- Brest is substantially more maritime: Météo-France 1991–2020 normals show roughly **1174 mm annual precipitation** and about **130 precipitation days**, so a western-coast buffer remains sensible even in the best months.
- WMO/Météo-France historical Brest monthly climatology shows July/August as drier than September and February markedly wetter/cooler, matching the route scores above.

## Activity-specific advice

- **Étretat / Cap Fréhel / Crozon:** wind matters as much as rain. Swap days if a frontal system arrives.
- **Mont-Saint-Michel:** tide timing can matter more than weather; choose an early/late visit around your preferred tide state.
- **Bruges/Ghent:** rain is much less damaging to trip value because museums/churches/covered cafés provide backup.
- **D-Day coast:** overcast is not a deal-breaker; strong wind/heavy rain is more disruptive.
- **Carnac/Vannes:** September remains very good because city/archaeology time is less heat-dependent than beach time.

## Recommendation

If choosing freely, target **roughly 3–30 September** and keep **one uncommitted Brittany night/day**. If you care more about maximum daylight than crowds/cost, choose **early to mid July**.
