# Cuba Speedrun: preserve the S/A sights, compress slack

**Theoretical minimum: 21 calendar days** (aggressive guaranteed road legs + precisely aligned Santiago–Havana flight, no disruption). **Recommended practical minimum: 23 days**, including arrival and return flight, with a protected Havana night before departure. Given 2026 fuel shortages and Austria's eastern-province warnings, **neither is currently actionable**. Do not reinterpret an unconfirmed domestic flight as a valid speedrun link.

The Speedrun **retains virtually every fixed-route S/A attraction** from the 28-day full route, including Camagüey, Sierra Maestra, Santiago, El Yunque and Alejandro de Humboldt. It reduces B-tier leisure, recovery and repeat visits. The separately noted specialist Jardines de la Reina liveaboard (A-tier) is *not part of the full fixed route*; add an expedition if it is a personal priority.

**Opening times are target blocks**, not confirmed ticket slots. Museum, guide, and coach schedules need checking again shortly before booking. Leave at least 45–60 minutes for meals and assume luggage storage in a confirmed casa before day sightseeing rather than nonexistent paid lockers.

### Theoretical 21-day compression

If all long transport pieces connect unusually well: remove (1) Havana's extra urban half-day by doing the city on arrival + early next day, and (2) shorten the eastern return buffer only with a **confirmed flight that reaches Havana the evening before international departure**. This is physically possible in an ideal operating network, but high cancellation exposure makes it a bad default. Never try to replace the 17-hour land crossing with a fictitious 2-hour guaranteed flight.

### Weather and disruption protection

Use the flexible window in order: **Humboldt / El Yunque**, then **Topes/El Nicho**, then **Zapata snorkeling**. The more realistic 23-day version retains a protected Havana exit night; adding 1–2 days beyond that becomes sensible if guide reservations or a scheduled coach cannot be secured.
