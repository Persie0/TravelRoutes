# Weather and season scoring for Cuba

**Best overall:** February and March. **Best-value shoulder:** late November (only after verifying hurricane aftermath and repairs) or early April. **Avoid for fixed-location speedruns:** September–October (storm/hurricane exposure); also June–August are humid/hot with thunderstorms. November–April is broadly the dry season; hurricane season runs 1 June–30 November. Scores are editorial estimates of *sightseeing suitability*, not measured averages or an operational safety rating. [Travel season guide](https://www.lonelyplanet.com/articles/best-time-to-visit-cuba), [hurricane and climate context](https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/travel-advisories/cuba.html).

- **February:** driest/hiking-friendly overall; occasional cooler evenings; southern-coast snorkeling can be excellent but wind/sea state always wins over averages.
- **March:** similar with slightly warmer temperatures; typically excellent mix for history, hiking, beach.
- **April:** warm and often still dry, stronger sun/heat; acceptable shoulder value.
- **June–November:** Atlantic hurricane season, with the most intense risk usually around August–October. Weather uncertainty makes Baracoa, Humboldt, boat outings and inland waterfall access unsuitable for a no-buffer schedule.
- **Baracoa:** local orographic rainfall can occur in the dry season; place a movable buffer around Humboldt/El Yunque.
- **Operational caveat:** season scores assume normal infrastructure. The fuel/electricity/medical shortages as of 2026 can make any month unsuitable; check BMEIA immediately before paying.

## Weather dependencies for Speedrun

Keep the flexible day first for **Baracoa + Alejandro de Humboldt** (access/river levels/landslides), second for **Topes de Collantes / El Nicho**, third for **Zapata snorkeling**. If no buffer exists, swap outdoor days with city walking only if transport allows it.
