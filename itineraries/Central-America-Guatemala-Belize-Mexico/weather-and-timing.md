## Why February/March win

### Guatemala highlands / Acatenango

For this route, cloud and rain matter more than warm temperatures. The whole point of the Acatenango overnight is the view toward Fuego, sunset/night visibility and a sunrise summit.

February and March strongly favor the dry-season side of the year. February can be **cold and windy at altitude**, but cold + clear is generally preferable to warm + cloud/rain for this particular experience.

**Important:** volcanic eruptions are not seasonal guarantees. Weather scores only represent the chance of better hiking/visibility conditions.

### Belize

Belize's National Meteorological Service describes two main seasons:

- **rainy:** most annual rainfall falls **June–November**;
- **dry:** roughly **November–May**, with April the driest month;
- the November–February part of dry season is cooler and can be affected by cold fronts;
- March–May is the warmer dry period.

That makes February–April particularly strong for Cayo + reef travel.

Official climate source: https://nms.gov.bz/climate-services/climate-summary/

### Hurricane/cyclone risk

NOAA states the official **Atlantic hurricane season is June 1–November 30**. Its climatology shows a sharp September peak, with most activity between roughly mid-August and mid-October.

This matters directly for Belize, Bacalar, Cancún and any boat/diving plans.

NOAA sources:
- https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/climo/
- https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/marinersguide.pdf

This does **not** mean a hurricane will occur on your trip in those months; it means the seasonal risk and disruption probability are materially higher.

## February-specific notes

### Acatenango

**Score: 10/10 for timing, but pack for winter-like summit conditions.**

Bring/ensure the operator supplies:
- warm insulation layers;
- wind protection;
- hat/gloves;
- appropriate sleeping setup;
- headlamp.

Do not look at Antigua's daytime temperature and assume the mountain camp will feel similar.

### Lake Atitlán

Generally one of the best times for clearer views. Mornings are usually the best time for calmer water and long-distance visibility, so schedule important boat/viewpoint activities earlier.

### Semuc Champey

Drier-season timing helps road and trail logistics. Water color can still change with local rainfall, so no month guarantees the exact turquoise appearance seen in marketing photos.

### Tikal

February is excellent for walking archaeological sites before the hotter months. Start early anyway: less heat, fewer crowds and better wildlife activity.

### Belize reef

February is excellent overall but **cold fronts can produce wind/chop**. This is why the 28-day itinerary intentionally includes a flexible Caye Caulker day rather than booking every reef activity back-to-back with zero recovery space.

### Bacalar / Yucatán

Warm without the worst late-spring heat. Bacalar's federal tourism page lists **Carnaval in February**, so exact dates are worth checking when the annual schedule is published.

Official Bacalar reference: https://www.gob.mx/sectur/articulos/bacalar-quintana-roo?idiom=es

## Seasonal recommendation

### Tier S — February, March

Pick whichever has the better airfare. Weather difference is too small to justify a major flight premium.

### Tier A — January, December, April

Still excellent. January/December can be cooler/windier; April is hotter.

### Tier B — November

Good shoulder-season candidate for lower prices, with more uncertainty than dry-season core months.

### Tier C — May, July, August

Possible, but increasingly wet/humid and less reliable for the route's headline experiences.

### Tier D — June, October

Would not be my first choice for a once-a-year 3–4 week trip.

### Tier F — September

The least attractive month for this exact combination of volcano views + roads + reef + Yucatán.
