## International airfare shells to test

### First choice — open jaw

Search as one multi-city ticket:

**VIE or BUD → Lima (LIM), around 1–2 Feb 2027**  
**Santiago (SCL) → VIE or BUD, around 3 Mar 2027**

Do not assume VIE must be used in both directions; also price BUD outbound/VIE inbound and the reverse if the booking engine allows separate tickets at sensible connection risk.

### Snapshot anchors, 27 Aug 2026

These are **indicative market anchors**, not an exact quote for the open-jaw combination:

- Vienna–Lima returns have recently appeared around **€760** on Skyscanner; February was shown as the cheapest month in the snapshot.
- Budapest–Lima was around **€845** in current Skyscanner results.
- Budapest–Santiago was around **€817** return in current February results.
- Vienna–Santa Cruz de la Sierra was roughly **€956+** return in the same research pass, making Bolivia itself a weaker long-haul gateway.
- Madrid–Santa Cruz has nonstop Air Europa / Boliviana de Aviación service, so separate VIE/BUD→MAD positioning is the main fallback worth testing.

Treat any exact fare as stale the moment inventory changes. Compare the **complete trip cost**, including checked baggage, airport transfers, an overnight buffer required by a separate ticket, and the lost day from backtracking.

## Decision rule

If the VIE/BUD→LIM // SCL→VIE/BUD open-jaw costs no more than roughly **€150–250 more** than the best sensible same-airport round trip, take the open jaw. A nominally cheaper Lima return can easily lose that saving once SCL→LIM positioning, baggage, connection buffers and a hotel are included.

## Internal bookings

- **LIM→AQP:** book a morning/early-afternoon flight rather than spending ~a full day on a long-distance bus.
- **La Paz→Sucre:** current Rome2Rio route data shows a flight around **1 h 07** versus a bus around **9 h 30**. Reconfirm that the flight actually operates on 15 Feb before building non-refundable connections around it.
- **Potosí→Uyuni:** current Rome2Rio route data shows bus around **3 h 56**; this is efficient enough to keep overland.
- **Uyuni→San Pedro 3-day tour:** reserve with a reputable operator, explicitly confirming Hito Cajón border transfer, wet-season route changes, oxygen/emergency equipment and what happens if the crossing temporarily closes.
- **San Pedro→Calama:** bus roughly 1.5 h class; leave margin before the flight.
- **CJC→SCL:** frequent nonstop domestic route, roughly 2 h.

## Uyuni booking tactic

Book the multi-day expedition, but keep the standalone mirror excursion locally flexible if possible. On arrival on 20 Feb, ask several operators where the best shallow water is and whether wind is expected to drop around sunrise/sunset. The route into the Salar can change substantially after heavy rain.

## Recheck checklist 2–4 weeks before departure

- Bolivia roadblock / protest situation.
- Hito Cajón border status and crossing hours.
- La Paz→Sucre flight schedule.
- Uyuni→San Pedro operator's wet-season plan.
- Short-range Uyuni rainfall/wind forecast.
- San Pedro altiplano access after summer storms.
- Travel-insurance coverage for activities above 4,000–5,000 m.
