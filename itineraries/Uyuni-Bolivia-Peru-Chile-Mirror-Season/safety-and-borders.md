# Safety, altitude and borders

Research snapshot: **27 August 2026**. This file is deliberately dated because security conditions and roadblocks can change quickly.

## Current government-advice snapshot

### Bolivia

Austria's BMEIA travel information, updated **12 August 2026**, currently assigns:

- **Safety level 3 / high security risk** to the La Paz/El Alto, Cochabamba and Santa Cruz areas;
- **Safety level 2** to the remainder of Bolivia.

The advisory highlights political/social tension, demonstrations and roadblocks, robbery/scam risk and problems around bus terminals. It advises avoiding demonstrations, using licensed operators/taxis and avoiding night overland bus travel.

This route therefore keeps La Paz but treats it as a place requiring active current checks rather than casual late-night transport.

### Peru

Austria currently uses level 2 for the relevant Peru travel context; normal urban crime, demonstrations and transport disruption still require awareness.

### Chile

Austria currently uses level 2 for Chile. San Pedro is tourist-oriented but remote excursions still require reputable operators and altitude/weather preparation.

## Bolivia operating rules for this itinerary

1. Check BMEIA and current roadblock information before each long transfer.
2. Avoid political demonstrations even if they appear peaceful.
3. Prefer daytime buses and flights over night coaches.
4. Use reputable licensed 4×4 operators for Uyuni/Sur Lípez.
5. Do not hand passports or valuables to unofficial 'police' or intermediaries without verifying identity.
6. Use official/known taxis or app-based transport where available for terminals and airports.
7. Build enough schedule slack that a roadblock does not force risky travel decisions.

## Altitude progression

Approximate elevations:

| Stop | Elevation class | Route implication |
|---|---:|---|
| Lima | sea level | recovery from long haul |
| Arequipa | ~2,300 m | first acclimatization step |
| Colca high passes | >4,500 m transient | take it slowly; short exposure |
| Puno / Titicaca | ~3,800 m | sustained high altitude begins |
| La Paz | ~3,600 m; El Alto >4,000 m | avoid hard exercise on arrival |
| Sucre | ~2,800 m | partial recovery |
| Potosí | ~4,000 m | very high city |
| Uyuni | ~3,650 m | high base |
| Sur Lípez | commonly 4,300–4,900+ m | hardest altitude section |
| San Pedro | ~2,400 m | major descent / recovery |

The Peru-first route is valuable because it avoids a direct sea-level → La Paz/Uyuni → 4,800 m jump.

### Altitude safety

Acute mountain sickness can become dangerous. Ascend gradually, stay hydrated, avoid hard exertion/alcohol early, and know emergency descent thresholds. A headache plus nausea/fatigue can be early AMS; confusion, inability to walk straight or breathlessness at rest are emergency warning signs requiring immediate descent/medical help. Check that travel insurance covers planned excursions above 4,000–5,000 m.

## Peru → Bolivia border

The Puno→Copacabana land crossing is the most natural one for this route. Carry:

- passport with sufficient validity;
- proof of onward travel;
- insurance evidence;
- accommodation details;
- any entry/exit record issued at the border.

Do not assume a bus waits indefinitely if immigration is delayed; understand the operator's border procedure before departure.

## Bolivia → Chile: Hito Cajón

The Uyuni→San Pedro 4×4 route normally exits Bolivia at **Hito Cajón**, a remote crossing around 4,500 m. The tour operator should explicitly include border transport and explain who transfers you on the Chilean side.

### Do not do this

Do **not** book a tight Calama flight on the afternoon of the border crossing. Wet-season roads, immigration and vehicle changes can all create delay. Sleep in San Pedro first.

## Wet-season closure contingency

If heavy rain temporarily prevents the normal Hito Cajón crossing:

1. follow the operator / border authority decision;
2. never attempt a closed track independently;
3. use the built-in San Pedro/Santiago buffer if the crossing is delayed;
4. if the border is expected to remain unusable, return to Uyuni and reposition by domestic air/road rather than risking an unofficial crossing.

## Cerro Rico mine tour

This is optional, not a must-do. The experience is historically unique but involves dust, confined spaces, altitude and an active mining environment. If participating, choose a reputable operator with helmets/lamps/PPE and be prepared to skip it if respiratory conditions, safety practices or current mine stability are poor.

## Night-arrival avoidance

Because Bolivia has both terminal crime risk and a current recommendation against night buses, schedule:

- La Paz arrival before very late night when possible;
- Sucre→Potosí by day;
- Potosí→Uyuni by day;
- licensed transfer to/from terminals rather than walking with luggage after dark.
