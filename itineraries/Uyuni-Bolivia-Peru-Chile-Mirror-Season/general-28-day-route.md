# General ~30-day optimized route

This is the evergreen first-trip version. It is intentionally about **30 days rather than forcing 28**, because the main objective—seeing Salar de Uyuni with standing water—benefits disproportionately from one weather buffer day.

## Route at a glance

**Lima → Arequipa → Colca Canyon → Puno → Copacabana / Isla del Sol → La Paz → Tiwanaku → Sucre → Potosí → Uyuni → Salar de Uyuni → Eduardo Avaroa / Sur Lípez → San Pedro de Atacama → Calama → Santiago**

## Why not fly directly to Uyuni or La Paz first?

For a one-month first trip, entering via Lima gives three advantages: a stronger international fare market, a gentler altitude ramp, and a linear route through several top-tier Andes sights. Starting immediately at 3,600–4,000+ m saves calendar time but increases the chance that altitude symptoms compromise the first several days.

## Why continue to Chile instead of returning to Uyuni?

The strongest Bolivia 4×4 circuit naturally continues south-west to **Hito Cajón and San Pedro de Atacama**. Returning to Uyuni repeats many hours of rough-road travel. Continuing into Chile converts the same expedition into a border-crossing transfer and puts the trip near Calama, a well-connected domestic airport for Santiago.

## Mirror-first operating rule

Do not pre-commit the one dedicated Uyuni day to a rigid exact sequence. Ask operators locally about water depth, wind and accessible sectors. A thin layer of water plus calm conditions is ideal; deep flooding can make some classic island sectors inaccessible. Sunset and very early morning often give calmer wind than midday.

## Buffer rule

If weather or blockades consume one day, cut **one Santiago city day or one San Pedro excursion**, not the dedicated Salar mirror day. Uyuni is the trip-defining objective.
