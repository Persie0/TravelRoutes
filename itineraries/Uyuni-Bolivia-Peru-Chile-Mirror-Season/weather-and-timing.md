# Weather and timing

## Bottom line

For this trip's unusual objective—**see Uyuni with shallow standing water while still completing the Sur Lípez crossing**—the best compromise is **late February into early March**.

- **Best pure mirror odds:** February.
- **Best mirror + route-reliability compromise:** roughly the last 10 days of February into the first 10 days of March.
- **Best dry-season road reliability:** May–October, but the classic water mirror usually disappears.
- **Best Atacama conditions alone:** roughly April–June and again around September–October.

The mirror is weather-dependent, not calendar-guaranteed. You need recent rain, shallow standing water and low wind at the same time.

## Uyuni: what actually creates the mirror

Wet-season guidance consistently places the most reliable mirror window around **January to early March**, with February typically the strongest month. But:

- rain by itself is not enough;
- wind can destroy the reflection even when water is present;
- too much water can close access to Isla Incahuasi and some classic routes;
- local water depth varies enormously across the Salar;
- one clear calm evening can be better than a brighter but windy midday.

### Practical tactic

Stay in/near Uyuni at least two nights before the multi-day crossing. Use the first afternoon to ask operators where water is best, then keep the next day dedicated to reflections. Prefer an itinerary that can include sunset; optionally add a sunrise/star-reflection departure if conditions are calm.

## Sur Lípez / Hito Cajón risk

The same summer rain that produces the mirror can flood or degrade high-altiplano tracks. December–March is the higher-risk period for route changes and temporary problems. That is why this itinerary:

- does not attach a same-day Calama flight to the border crossing;
- gives San Pedro a recovery/buffer day;
- keeps Santiago as an additional downstream buffer.

## Atacama's 'Altiplanic winter'

Northern Chile can receive high-altitude summer storms during roughly December–March. San Pedro itself is extremely dry, but excursions to Piedras Rojas, altiplanic lagoons and geysers depend on mountain-road conditions. A rainy-season Bolivia + Chile trip therefore needs more excursion flexibility than an April–June trip.

## Alternative timing profiles

### Maximum mirror probability

**1–28 February.** Best for reflections; accept the greatest route disruption risk.

### Recommended compromise

**15 February–15 March.** Put Uyuni in the last third of February or very early March. Slightly better chance of stable onward roads while preserving good mirror odds.

### Stable roads, no mirror priority

**May–June or September.** Much more reliable Altiplano travel, crisp skies and better hiking/photography, but normally a dry white Salar rather than the giant reflection.

## Months to avoid for this exact goal

No month is inherently unsafe simply because of weather, but **June–September** are poor choices if standing water is a core requirement. Conversely, **January–February** are poor choices if your priority is a guaranteed fixed 4×4 route with no weather-driven changes.
