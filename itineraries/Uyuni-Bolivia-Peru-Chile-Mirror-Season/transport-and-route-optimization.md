# Transport and route optimization

Research snapshot: **27 August 2026**. Timetables and political/road conditions in Bolivia can change; recheck close to travel.

## Optimized spine

**Lima → Arequipa → Colca → Puno → Copacabana → La Paz → Sucre → Potosí → Uyuni → Salar / Sur Lípez → Hito Cajón → San Pedro → Calama → Santiago**

The route is almost continuously southeast. The only deliberate out-and-back is **La Paz ↔ Tiwanaku**, a short high-value archaeological day trip.

## Major legs

| Leg | Preferred mode | Approx. time | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Lima → Arequipa | flight | ~1.5 h airborne | avoids consuming nearly a full day overland |
| Arequipa/Colca → Puno | tourist/regular bus | ~6 h class | scenic linear highland transfer |
| Puno → Copacabana | bus | ~5–6 h incl. border/stop pattern | simplest Peru→Bolivia continuation |
| Copacabana → La Paz | bus | ~4–5 h | direct continuation; avoid very late arrival |
| La Paz ↔ Tiwanaku | day tour / road | ~1.5–2 h each way | high-value UNESCO day trip |
| La Paz → Sucre | **flight if operating/sensible** | Rome2Rio currently ~1 h 07 | buys back most of a sightseeing day; bus ~9 h 30 |
| Sucre → Potosí | bus | ~3 h | short, frequent highland corridor |
| Potosí → Uyuni | bus | **~3 h 56** current Rome2Rio | efficient; no need to fly/backtrack |
| Uyuni → San Pedro | 3-day 4×4 | 3 days / 2 nights | combines transport with the trip's strongest landscapes |
| San Pedro → Calama | bus | ~1.5 h | straightforward airport connection |
| Calama → Santiago | nonstop flight | ~2 h | frequent domestic route; far better than long overland travel |

## Why not use a La Paz → Uyuni flight in the full route?

It is excellent in the **12-day 60:40 route**, but in a month-long route it would skip Sucre and Potosí or force backtracking. The Sucre→Potosí→Uyuni chain is geographically coherent and adds two UNESCO/history-heavy stops for relatively modest transfer time.

## Why not finish back in La Paz or Lima?

A conventional Uyuni circuit often returns to Uyuni after the lagoons, forcing you north again and then onto another flight. Ending the expedition at **Hito Cajón / San Pedro de Atacama** converts the excursion into useful transport. From San Pedro, Calama gives frequent access to Santiago, one of South America's major international gateways.

## Border crossings

### Peru → Bolivia

The Puno/Copacabana corridor is the natural tourist crossing for this route. Allow contingency for immigration queues, strikes or temporary roadblocks. Keep proof of onward travel and insurance accessible.

### Bolivia → Chile at Hito Cajón

Hito Cajón is the standard high-altitude crossing used by Uyuni–San Pedro expeditions. It sits above 4,500 m and is remote. Do not schedule a tight Calama flight on the same day. The full itinerary sleeps in San Pedro first.

## Wet-season transport risk

January–March rain is exactly what creates the Uyuni mirror, but it is also the route's largest operational risk. Heavy rain can:

- make Isla Incahuasi or particular salt-flat sectors inaccessible;
- alter the 4×4 itinerary;
- slow desert tracks;
- cause temporary problems on the Hito Cajón approach;
- affect Atacama altiplano roads during the Chilean 'Altiplanic winter'.

The itinerary therefore carries buffer in La Paz, Uyuni and San Pedro rather than scheduling every night as a hard connection.

## Night-bus policy

Austria's BMEIA currently advises against night overland bus travel in Bolivia. For this itinerary:

- prefer the La Paz→Sucre flight over an overnight coach;
- do Sucre→Potosí and Potosí→Uyuni in daylight;
- avoid arriving at isolated terminals late at night;
- use licensed operators/taxis for terminal transfers.

## Transfer-value audit

### Worth paying extra

- Lima→Arequipa flight.
- La Paz→Sucre flight when schedule is good.
- Calama→Santiago flight.
- La Paz→Uyuni flight in the 60:40 route.

### Not worth paying extra

- Sucre→Potosí: bus is short enough.
- Potosí→Uyuni: current bus time under 4 h is efficient.
- San Pedro→Calama: road bus is already fast.

## Contingency hierarchy

If a roadblock/weather disruption costs time, cut in this order:

1. Lima extra sightseeing;
2. Santiago extra sightseeing;
3. one secondary San Pedro excursion;
4. optional Cerro Rico tour;
5. La Paz secondary nature excursion.

Do **not** cut the dedicated Uyuni mirror day unless there is no alternative.
