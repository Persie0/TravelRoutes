# Worthwhile variants

## 24–25 days — recommended shorter version

Cut: Newcastle full day, St Andrews overnight, Aberdeen city day (keep Dunnottar only if easy), one Assynt/Torridon day, one Skye day, one Edinburgh buffer. Do **not** cut both Torridon and Glencoe just to keep more cities.

## 35–38 days — best longer version

Add **one** island region properly:

### Orkney (+3–4 days)
Best for archaeology/history: Skara Brae, Ring of Brodgar, Maeshowe, dramatic coast. Geometrically pairs with a fuller NC500 north/east arc.

### Lewis & Harris (+4–5 days)
Best for Gaelic culture, beaches and Atlantic landscapes. Ferry from Ullapool to Stornoway makes it a natural extension before Torridon/Skye, but ferry/accommodation planning becomes more important.

## 40–45 days — deep Scotland version

Add both Orkney and Lewis/Harris, slow the Highland hiking sections, and give Skye 4–5 nights. At this point the trip becomes Scotland-heavy rather than “London + rail cities + Highlands”.

## August festival version

Keep the route order, but engineer Edinburgh to land during the Fringe / International Festival / Tattoo period. Book Edinburgh accommodation dramatically earlier than for July/September. If festival shows matter, move one London day and one Skye day to Edinburgh rather than adding days blindly.

## Hiking-heavy version

- Cairngorms: 2 full mountain days
- Assynt: Stac Pollaidh + Suilven
- Torridon: 2 days
- Skye: 4 days including Cuillin/Loch Coruisk
- Glencoe: 2 days

This pushes the trip toward 34–36 days if city content is preserved.

## Budget version

- September over August
- Hostels/guesthouses outside Portree and central Edinburgh
- Book rail Advance fares at release
- Compare Railcard eligibility
- Keep one-way compact rental car, but price the Aberdeen→Edinburgh drop fee against renting Inverness→Edinburgh
- Use the Skye Bridge if ferry + reservation costs are unattractive
