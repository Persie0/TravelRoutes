## Recommendation

### 1) August — best overall

Best compromise for a one-month trip: long daylight, prime hiking season, essentially all normal summer infrastructure open, and slightly less pressure than peak July. Northern Norway no longer has true midnight sun for most of August, but nights are still bright early in the month.

### 2) July — best for maximum daylight and road reliability

Choose July if you care most about hiking flexibility, late-night photography and the highest chance that seasonal roads/ferries are operating. Trade-off: busiest month and typically the most expensive rental/accommodation period.

### 3) September — best atmosphere, but reverse the trip

September gives autumn colour, fewer people, and northern-lights potential in the north. But a top-to-bottom route reaches high mountain roads late in the month exactly when closure risk rises.

**September direction: Stavanger → Bergen → Sognefjord → Geiranger/Loen → Trondheim → train to Bodø → Lofoten → Senja/Tromsø.**

That protects Gamle Strynefjellsvegen/Aurlandsfjellet early and ends in Northern Norway when darkness improves aurora odds.

## Weather reality

Fjord Norway has a coastal climate: rain and wind can change quickly. Northern Norway can give 'four seasons in one day'. Build at least:
- 1 weather-flex day in Lofoten;
- 1 flex day around Geiranger/Loen;
- waterproof shell + warm midlayer even in July/August.

## Seasonal facts to re-check

- Tromsø main hiking season is generally **June–October**, depending on snow/weather.
- Midnight sun in Tromsø is roughly **late May–late July**.
- Fjord summer high season is **June–August**.
- Gamle Strynefjellsvegen is winter-closed and can close during September in some years.
