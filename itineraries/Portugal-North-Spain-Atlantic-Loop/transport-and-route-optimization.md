# Transport and route optimization

## Portugal without a car

Rome2Rio checks made during research:

| Leg | Best practical mode | Approx time | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Faro → Lagos | Bus | **~1h20** | Faster than the train (~1h41) in the checked routing |
| Lagos → Lisbon | Bus | **~3h40** | Train routing was ~5h04 in the check |
| Lisbon → Coimbra | Train | **~1h36** | Clear time win over bus (~2h20) |
| Coimbra → Porto | Train | **~1h01** | Fast, city-centre friendly |

Portugal’s official tourism information lists Alfa Pendular/InterCity as the backbone for Lisbon–Porto and gives indicative rail fares; book Comboios de Portugal directly when schedules open.

## Why not rent in Faro?
- Parking/traffic adds friction in Lisbon and Porto.
- Faro/Lagos/Lisbon/Coimbra/Porto are unusually easy to link without a car.
- One long rental from Porto onward makes cross-border paperwork simpler and concentrates the cost where it materially improves the trip.

## Car section

**Recommended pickup/return:** Porto → Porto, same country and same station if possible. That avoids the large international one-way drop fees commonly attached to Portugal→Spain rentals.

Route: **Porto → Viana → Vigo → Santiago → Costa da Morte → A Coruña → Ribadeo → Asturias/Picos → Santander → Bilbao → Burgos → León → Bragança → Douro → Porto.**

This is more efficient than driving Bilbao → Porto directly and then adding separate inland day trips: the return becomes a sightseeing corridor.

### Cross-border rental checklist
- Explicitly choose a rental rate that permits **Portugal + Spain** travel.
- Tell the rental company you will cross the border; some contracts require written authorization/fee.
- Confirm electronic toll handling for Portugal (rental transponder/service).
- Keep registration/rental agreement and roadside-assistance number in the car.
- Do not assume the cheapest broker rate includes cross-border permission.

## Critical timed-access items
- **Cíes Islands:** ferry + national-park authorization/seasonal capacity; book in advance.
- **Praia das Catedrais:** official Galicia tourism says authorization is required **July 1–September 30** and beach access is only meaningful at low tide.
- **Covadonga/Lakes / Ruta del Cares:** Asturias uses seasonal shuttle/access controls; verify the exact 2027 rules before driving up.
- **Gaztelugatxe:** Basque tourism currently states a free access ticket is required on controlled dates.

## Bottlenecks
1. As Catedrais tide can force a schedule shuffle.
2. Picos rain/cloud can kill a planned hike; keep Covadonga/Cares swappable.
3. Cíes ferry can be cancelled or sell out.
4. Northern coastal driving is slow once you deliberately leave motorways for viewpoints and villages.
