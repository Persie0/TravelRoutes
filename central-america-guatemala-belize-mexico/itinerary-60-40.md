# 60:40 highlights route — roughly 60% of the experience value in ~40% of the time

This is **not** a mechanically shortened 28-day itinerary. It is a value-per-day optimization.

The full route spends time on beautiful but expensive detours (especially Semuc Champey and the Belize→Bacalar→Valladolid overland chain). The 60:40 route deliberately spends money on two strategic flights so the time saved can be converted into S-tier experiences.

## Default 60:40 route

**Guatemala City → Antigua → Acatenango/Fuego → GUA airport → fly to Flores → Tikal → San Ignacio → ATM Cave → Xunantunich if border timing allows → Belize City → Caye Caulker → Hol Chan/Shark Ray Alley → Belize City → fly to Cancún → Valladolid → Chichén Itzá → Cancún**

Target: **~12 days in-region** (fly home on Day 13 if needed).

### Why these cuts?

The condensed route protects the five experiences with the strongest combination of uniqueness, sightseeing value and route impact:

1. Acatenango/Fuego
2. Tikal
3. ATM Cave
4. Hol Chan/Shark Ray Alley
5. Chichén Itzá

It also keeps Antigua because Acatenango naturally starts there, Caye Caulker because it is the best reef base, and San Ignacio because ATM requires it.

The first things removed are **Semuc Champey, Bacalar, Yaxhá, Ek Balam, Caracol, the Lake Atitlán multi-day stay and generic Cancún beach time**. These are good-to-excellent experiences, but their value-per-transfer-hour is lower in a 12-day constraint.

## Weighted-value estimate

Using the normalized `data/sights.csv` experience-point model, the 60:40 route retains roughly **63% of the scored experience value** while using about **43% of the 28-day time budget**. Exact percentages depend on whether Xunantunich fits on Day 6.

This model is intentionally qualitative, not pseudo-scientific: it is a disciplined way to stop low-priority detours from crowding out S-tier experiences.

## Two expensive time-saving moves

### 1. Guatemala City (GUA) → Flores (FRS)

Current route research (Aug 2026) shows nonstop service by **Avianca and TAG Airlines**, with flight times around **50–60 minutes** and multiple daily frequencies.

This bypasses the long northbound road journey and is the key reason the 60:40 route can keep Tikal.

### 2. Belize City (BZE) → Cancún (CUN)

Tropic Air currently operates the nonstop route, around **1h30**. Rome2Rio's route comparison from Caye Caulker to Cancún shows the fly option at about **4h43 overall**, versus roughly **9h21** ferry+train and **11h35** ferry+bus.

The flight is expensive relative to buses, but that is exactly the trade the 60:40 route makes: **money for a whole sightseeing day**.

## Budget 60:40 alternative

If internal flights are too expensive, do not try to keep every S-tier stop anyway.

Use a **Guatemala + Belize 11–12 day route**:

**Antigua → Acatenango → GUA→FRS flight → Tikal → San Ignacio/ATM → Caye Caulker/Hol Chan → depart Belize**

and save Yucatán for another trip.

Trying to do the same 60:40 itinerary entirely overland generally converts the holiday into transfer days.

## Landscape-biased 60:40 alternative

If volcanic/lake scenery matters more than Chichén:

- add **1–2 nights Lake Atitlán** after Acatenango;
- cut Valladolid/Chichén;
- depart from Belize or fly BZE→CUN only to connect home.

This version is less globally iconic but better balanced for nature-first travelers.
