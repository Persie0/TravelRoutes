# February 1–28, 2027 — detailed itinerary

This is the working schedule for using almost the entire month of February. It is intentionally fast-paced but keeps strategic buffer days around the two experiences most vulnerable to conditions: **Acatenango/Fuego** and **Belize reef trips**.

## Feb 1 — Europe → Guatemala City → Antigua

**Goal:** get to Antigua on arrival day if safely practical.

- GUA → Antigua is the logical first move; Guatemala City adds little to this route compared with Antigua.
- Rome2Rio route overview showed ~30 min for its fastest bus listing, ~40 min drive/taxi and ~90 min shuttle. Real airport/traffic times can be longer.
- If the long-haul flight arrives very late, sleep near GUA and transfer first thing Feb 2 rather than forcing a night road transfer.

**Sleep:** Antigua.

## Feb 2 — Antigua UNESCO historic centre

Priorities:

1. Parque Central / Cathedral area.
2. Santa Catalina Arch.
3. Iglesia de La Merced.
4. One or two major ruin complexes such as Santa Clara / Las Capuchinas.
5. Cerro de la Cruz late afternoon if visibility is good.

UNESCO notes Antigua's preserved Renaissance-influenced grid and important Baroque monuments/ruins, including the Cathedral, La Merced, Las Capuchinas and Santa Clara.

**Sleep:** Antigua.

## Feb 3–4 — Acatenango/Fuego overnight

This is the route's signature hike.

Typical structure:

- Feb 3 morning: operator pickup and trail start.
- Afternoon: reach high camp.
- Sunset/night: watch Volcán Fuego if activity/cloud allow.
- Optional Fuego saddle excursion: substantially harder; only do it if guide, fitness and conditions support it.
- Feb 4 pre-dawn: Acatenango summit attempt.
- Descend and return to Antigua around midday/afternoon.

### Why the next day stays flexible

The summit is high, nights can be cold/windy and the hike is demanding. Do not schedule an irreplaceable long-distance connection immediately after finishing.

**Sleep Feb 3:** mountain camp.  
**Sleep Feb 4:** Antigua.

## Feb 5 — Antigua recovery + weather buffer

Use this for:

- recovery/laundry;
- remaining Antigua sights;
- moving Acatenango by one day if weather/operator logistics require it.

If absolutely necessary, this is a cuttable day.

**Sleep:** Antigua.

## Feb 6 — Antigua → Lake Atitlán

- Transfer to Panajachel.
- Continue by boat if staying elsewhere on the lake.

### Base choice

- **San Juan La Laguna:** best cultural/art-oriented base.
- **Santa Cruz La Laguna:** excellent scenery/quiet.
- **Panajachel:** easiest transport/logistics, less atmospheric.
- **San Marcos:** wellness/retreat vibe.

For this itinerary I would stay **San Juan or Santa Cruz** and use boats rather than changing hotel every night.

**Sleep:** Lake Atitlán.

## Feb 7 — San Juan + lake villages

Priorities:

- San Juan La Laguna murals, local art/textile/co-op experiences.
- Boat to another village rather than trying to tick all of them.
- Good pairing: San Juan + Santiago Atitlán.

**Sleep:** Lake Atitlán.

## Feb 8 — slower Lake Atitlán day

Use the second full lake day for one of:

- Santiago Atitlán;
- Santa Catarina Palopó;
- kayaking / lakeside walking;
- a viewpoint hike with current local safety advice;
- simply keeping a slower day before the long transfer north.

This day makes the route feel like a 4-week trip rather than a transfer marathon.

**Sleep:** Lake Atitlán.

## Feb 9 — Lake Atitlán → Lanquín / Semuc Champey

**Critical optimization:** do **not** go back to Guatemala City just to head north again.

- Book a direct tourist shuttle/private transfer when dates are closer.
- Rome2Rio's routing for this leg is inconsistent and can resolve Atitlán to specific Panajachel properties, so use it for mode comparison only.
- Treat the entire day as transit.

**Sleep:** Lanquín or near Semuc Champey.

## Feb 10 — Semuc Champey

Non-negotiables:

1. Mirador hike first, before heat/fatigue.
2. Turquoise limestone pools and swimming.

Optional extras:

- K'an Ba cave-style tours;
- tubing.

Those extras are more operator/condition-dependent than the park itself. Do not let them crowd out the mirador and pools.

**Sleep:** Lanquín / Semuc.

## Feb 11 — Semuc Champey → Flores

Rome2Rio showed:

- bus ~440 min (~7 h 20 min);
- shuttle ~480 min (~8 h).

This is a pure transfer day. Buy water/snacks and expect road variability.

**Sleep:** Flores.

## Feb 12 — Tikal

Tikal deserves a full day. UNESCO describes it as one of the major Maya sites and a rare mixed World Heritage property recognized for both archaeology and biodiversity.

Priorities inside the site:

- Great Plaza;
- Temple I / Temple II area;
- Temple IV / major high viewpoints where access is permitted;
- Lost World complex;
- time for wildlife/forest rather than sprinting between structures.

### Sunrise vs normal early entry

Sunrise can be atmospheric, but cloud/fog and special-ticket logistics can reduce the payoff. A normal very-early start is often the better value unless sunrise itself is a priority.

**Sleep:** Flores.

## Feb 13 — Yaxhá sunset + Flores

If you want a second Maya complex, **Yaxhá is the best fit** because it offers a distinct forest/lake atmosphere and a famous sunset experience.

If archaeological saturation has set in, skip Yaxhá and spend the day around Flores/Lake Petén Itzá instead.

**Sleep:** Flores.

## Feb 14 — Flores → San Ignacio, Belize

Rome2Rio showed ~2 h 30 min for bus/shuttle routing, but border formalities mean you should allow substantially more door-to-door time.

- Cross at the Guatemala–Belize land border.
- Keep passport/entry documentation accessible.
- Do not book a tightly timed afternoon tour.

**Sleep:** San Ignacio.

## Feb 15 — ATM Cave

**Actun Tunichil Muknal is the #1 San Ignacio experience.** Belize's official tourism site describes a drive, jungle hike with stream crossings, swim into the cave and guided exploration of Maya ceramics and human remains.

This is a guided-only, physically active day. Expect water, scrambling and strict preservation rules.

**Sleep:** San Ignacio.

## Feb 16 — Xunantunich + Cahal Pech

### Morning: Xunantunich

Official Belize tourism highlights:

- hand-cranked ferry across the Mopan River;
- six plazas;
- 25+ temples/palaces;
- El Castillo around 130 ft above the plaza;
- broad Cayo/Guatemala views in clear weather.

### Afternoon: Cahal Pech

Use Cahal Pech as the compact local complement rather than another long transfer.

**Sleep:** San Ignacio.

## Feb 17 — Caracol + Mountain Pine Ridge OR flex day

This is the ambitious day.

**Option A — archaeology/nature maximum:** Caracol + Mountain Pine Ridge sights on an organized day trip.

**Option B — lower stress:** skip Caracol and use the day for Mountain Pine Ridge, local food, river activities or rest.

Because Tikal/Yaxhá/Xunantunich/Chichén are already in the route, Caracol is the first major archaeological site I would drop if you start feeling temple fatigue.

**Sleep:** San Ignacio.

## Feb 18 — San Ignacio → Belize City → Caye Caulker

Rome2Rio overview:

- San Ignacio → Belize City bus ~148 min;
- shuttle options roughly 120–145 min;
- Belize City → Caye Caulker ferry ~45 min.

**Optimization:** transfer through Belize City; don't automatically sleep there.

**Sleep:** Caye Caulker.

## Feb 19 — Hol Chan + Shark Ray Alley

Best reef day for most non-divers.

Belize tourism describes Hol Chan as Belize's first marine reserve, with coral reef, seagrass, mangrove and Shark Ray Alley zones. Shark Ray Alley is known for nurse sharks, rays and turtles.

Book a responsible licensed operator and follow reef-contact/wildlife rules.

**Sleep:** Caye Caulker.

## Feb 20 — Great Blue Hole / Lighthouse Reef

### If you are a diver

A full-day Lighthouse Reef / Great Blue Hole trip becomes one of the route's headline experiences.

### If you are not a diver

The Blue Hole is arguably more impressive **from the air** than as a snorkel-only objective. Alternatives:

- scenic flight;
- another high-quality reef snorkel day;
- relaxed island day.

Belize tourism notes the Great Blue Hole is roughly 300 m across and over 120 m deep, within Lighthouse Reef Atoll.

**Sleep:** Caye Caulker.

## Feb 21 — Caye Caulker weather buffer / island day

Keep this day. Reef tours can be disrupted by wind/sea conditions even in good season.

If Feb 19–20 both run perfectly, use Feb 21 for:

- cycling/walking the island;
- The Split / swimming;
- kayaking;
- relaxed food/bar day;
- another reef activity.

**Sleep:** Caye Caulker.

## Feb 22 — Caye Caulker → Bacalar, Mexico

Rome2Rio's fastest listed combination was ~312 min (~5 h 12 min), with other ferry/bus combinations around 5.5–7 h.

Allow more time than the headline number for:

- boat connections;
- Belize exit;
- Mexico entry;
- road schedules.

This is still far better than trying to continue all the way to Valladolid in one ~10 h transport chain.

**Sleep:** Bacalar.

## Feb 23 — Bacalar

Mexico's federal tourism page highlights:

- Laguna de Bacalar / “Lagoon of Seven Colors”;
- Cenote Azul;
- Fuerte de San Felipe;
- Museo de la Piratería;
- central plaza.

Suggested day:

- sunrise / early water time;
- low-impact kayak/sailing/lagoon tour;
- Fort San Felipe in the afternoon;
- sunset by the lagoon.

The official tourism page also lists **Carnaval in February**, so check 2027 dates once published.

**Sleep:** Bacalar.

## Feb 24 — Bacalar → Valladolid

Rome2Rio: bus ~284 min (~4 h 44 min).

After arrival:

- Zócalo;
- San Servacio;
- Calzada de los Frailes;
- evening food walk.

**Sleep:** Valladolid.

## Feb 25 — Chichén Itzá + cenote

Use Valladolid to beat the Cancún day-trip wave.

Current INAH information (research snapshot 2026-08-23):

- opening 08:00–16:00;
- last access 15:00;
- separate federal/state components of the admission fee apply.

Arrive close to opening. Priorities:

- El Castillo / Kukulkán pyramid;
- Great Ball Court;
- Temple of the Warriors;
- Sacred Cenote;
- observatory / additional complexes if time allows.

Add a cenote after the archaeological zone rather than trying to rush Chichén itself.

**Sleep:** Valladolid.

## Feb 26 — Ek Balam + Valladolid

### Morning: Ek Balam

A strong final archaeology option because it is close to Valladolid and has a different character from Chichén.

### Afternoon/evening: Valladolid

Mexico's federal tourism page lists:

- Cenote Zací;
- Ex-Convento de San Bernardino de Siena;
- Calzada de los Frailes;
- Casa de los Venados;
- churches / Zócalo / local markets and Yucatecan food.

**Sleep:** Valladolid.

## Feb 27 — Valladolid → Cancún

Rome2Rio overview:

- ADO bus ~160 min;
- Tren Maya ~176 min;
- drive ~106 min.

Use the rest of the day for:

- a beach;
- Isla Mujeres only if timings make sense;
- relaxing before the long-haul flight.

This is deliberately also the **international-flight buffer day**.

**Sleep:** Cancún.

## Feb 28 — Cancún → Europe

Current indicative winner for using the whole month:

- CUN → BUD Feb 28: ~€358 in the Skyscanner snapshot.

If a competitive CUN→VIE fare appears later, reassess total door-to-door cost rather than airfare alone.

# Optional Mexico City variant

If Mexico City matters more than Caye/Bacalar/Valladolid buffer time:

- reach Cancún around Feb 23;
- CUN → NLU/MEX: indicative from ~€38 on selected February dates;
- spend 2–3 nights in Mexico City;
- MEX → BUD Feb 26: indicative ~€424.

Compared with CUN→BUD Feb 28 (~€358), the observed return-side premium is roughly **€100+**, and you give up the final two February days.

For a first trip with only February available, I prefer the **full route without Mexico City**.

# What to cut if something goes wrong

Cut in this order:

1. Caracol.
2. Ek Balam.
3. Yaxhá.
4. Feb 5 Antigua recovery day if Acatenango runs on schedule.
5. One Lake Atitlán day.

Protect these as long as possible:

- Acatenango/Fuego;
- Tikal;
- ATM Cave;
- Hol Chan/Shark Ray Alley;
- Chichén Itzá;
- one proper Lake Atitlán day;
- Semuc Champey.
